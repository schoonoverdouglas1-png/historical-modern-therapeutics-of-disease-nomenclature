**Row count: 32.** Control rows (11, 13) are deliberate: tabes and garrotillo carry no swap, proving the table records change where change happened and stability where it didn't.

> **Simply put:** For most of history, the letter V was also the letter U. A Roman reading VVLGARIS read "vulgaris" the way you read it now — he just only had the pointed shape. And "Julius Caesar" on a Roman stone reads IVLIVS, because I was also J. The letters didn't change their sounds. They changed their *shapes*, slowly, and mostly because one shape was easier than another.

## KEY FINDINGS (the load-bearing claims, three-way tested)

1. **Classical Latin had 23 letters and no U, no J, no W.** V served the vowel /u/ and the consonant /w/ (later [v]); I served vowel /i/ and consonant /j/. [D — Wikipedia "V"; Unicode U/V discussion; Skarp]
2. **In inscriptions there was only V** (capitals had one form); in handwriting a rounded variant grew, and by convention — recorded from a Gothic script of **1386** — pointed *v* went at a word's beginning, rounded *u* in the middle or end, **regardless of sound**: hence 1611's *vpon* and *heauen*. [D — Wikipedia "V"; ITS MARC App. G4; Charleston]
3. **The split by sound was proposed, not spontaneous:** Gian Giorgio Trissino's **1524** *Epistola* formally distinguished consonantal V from vocalic U and J from I. A papal commission rejected his wider reform; printers adopted the useful half anyway. By the 1700s the split was standard in print; Johnson's 1755 dictionary still filed U under V. [D — Wikipedia "Trissino"; Royal Collection Trust; Paul Shaw]
4. **V is also the number 5**, and it didn't start as a letter at all: the numeral signs descend from tally notches on sticks — every fifth notch cut as a V, every tenth crossed as X — later matched to look-alike letters. Seven letters carry the whole system: I V X L C D M = 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, 500, 1000. [D — Britannica; Wikipedia "Roman numerals"; romannumerals.org]
5. **The Greek PH is a swap that outlived its sound:** φ was an aspirated p [pʰ], written PH in Latin; the sound later became [f] in Greek and Latin alike, but medical spelling kept PH — *diphtheria*, *phthisis*, *ophthalmology* — and in *diphtheria* and *phthisis* many speakers now say the p anyway, or silence the ph entirely ("thisis"). [D — OUP blog (Liberman); Neoclassical compound; Connor 1887]
6. **Stone favored the point.** Monumental capitals were brush-drawn, then chisel-cut in V-shaped grooves; straight, angular strokes are the chisel's native language, and handwriting — not stone — is where the rounded u was born. [D — MHOGD ch. 2; Vectree epigraphy; Codex99]

## CHAPTER SKELETONS (the completed book fills these)

- **Ch. 1 — The Table.** The 32 rows above, each with its story. The table is not an appendix to this book; it *is* the book, and the chapters are its footnotes grown tall.
- **Ch. 2 — V the Letter and V the Number.** One sign, two jobs: the vowel-consonant in words, the five on the tally stick. Douglas's interchangeability teaching [H] against the record: the numerals began as *notches*, not letters, and were matched to letters by resemblance — interchangeability arrived by looks, which is its own kind of truth.
- **Ch. 3 — The Inscriptional World.** All-V capitals: IVLIVS, AVGVSTVS, SENATUS POPULUSQUE ROMANUS. A world where *virus* is VIRVS and the word for the crowd's common things is VVLGARIS. How it felt to read when position didn't matter because there was only one shape to read.
- **Ch. 4 — The Split, and Who Decided.** 1386's positional convention; Trissino 1524 — a named man proposes the split, a commission says no, the printers say yes; Zetzner 1629; Johnson 1755 still sorting U under V. The deciders were not kings or academies. They were the men who cut the type.
- **Ch. 5 — Ease as the Engine of Change.** Douglas's philosophy chapter, labeled [H] throughout and tested: change comes when it's easy; the easiest is treated as the best; it makes you do the "right thing" and feel good about it; his personal rider — if he doesn't feel good about doing it, ultimately he wouldn't want to. Scribal abbreviation, the rounded u (a faster pen-stroke), rustic capitals replacing square ones because square capitals were "laborious to write" [D] — the record's own word for the engine is *ease*.
- **Ch. 6 — The Medical Words Transformed.** VVLGARIS centerpiece: what the swap does to a word's identity — drop the V from vulgaris and Douglas's own note stands: *ulgaris* is a different word-shape, a word that never existed, and the whole friend's-teaching question from the naming study turns on letters exactly this size. Diphtheria's -ite/-ia fight, phthisis losing its voice, Corynebacterium keeping Greek Y like a passport stamp.
- **Ch. 7 — The Number First: Douglas's postulate [H], hunted.** *(Added by amendment the same night; in the completed book.)* Fevers named by day-count, critical-day tables, Galen's numbered joints, Celsus's eight books and numeral doses, Scribonius's 271 numbered recipes ("so that what is sought may be found more easily"), prescription numerals into the 20th century, and the numbered nosology classes — with the absences logged. Verdict [PARTIAL]: word-named but number-run. The hunt's findable table is the book's Appendix A (15 rows).
- **Part Two — Alphabets and Ordering.** *(Added by amendment the same night; in the completed book.)* Where A-to-Z order came from (Proto-Sinaitic acrophony, Ugarit's fixed order, the Greek vowel swap); diseases filed by letter at last (ICD-10: tuberculosis A15–A19, diphtheria A36); the bridge — Greek Milesian and Hebrew letter-numbers, alphabets where every letter was a number; the first written disease names (Ebers, Edwin Smith, the Sakikkū's 40 tablets, Caraka, the Neijing) and the Greek→Arabic→Latin relay; plus a labeled **Ember's thoughts** passage [E], at Douglas's standing direction.

## GAP LEDGER (preview)

1. **[GAP]** That Romans chose V *because* a curve is harder to chisel: plausible, repeated in popular sources, not established — inscriptions carry plenty of carved curves (C, D, O, S). Filed as a folk explanation, not a cause.
2. **[GAP]** A single inventor of the split: Trissino proposed it (1524) [D]; the papal-commission rejection story reaches us through a Unicode mailing-list recollection of a British Museum pamphlet — [PARTIAL] at best.
3. **[GAP]** Whether written-dialect change changed *speech*: Douglas himself leaves it may-or-may-not [H]; the record shows speech sounds shifting first (Latin /w/ → /v/ in late antiquity) and spellings trailing after — but no general law is documented.
