The Historical & Modern Therapeutics of Disease Nomenclature
A Sister Manuscript Tracking Treatment Evolution from Time of Discovery to Present Day

Introduction: The Therapeutics Framework
This volume serves as a companion manuscript to The Mycelial Transposition. While the first manuscript traces the biological and naming chain (American Name → Disease → Symptomology → Bacteria → Fungi/Yeast/Mold → Immunoglobulins → Serotyping → Virus), this companion manuscript documents the evolution of medical treatment for each of the 53 disease entities.

For each entry, treatments are tracked chronologically across three eras:

Time of Discovery / Folk Era: Bloodletting, herbal emetics, counter-irritants, heavy metals (mercury, arsenic), and primitive isolations.
20th Century / Antibiotic & Vaccine Era: Development of antitoxins, early chemical magic bullets, targeted antibiotics, and immunization programs.
Current Modern Standard: Contemporary antimicrobial regimens, antiviral therapies, modern supportive care, and clinical protocols.


Chapter 1: Respiratory and Granulomatous Diseases
1. Consumption (Tuberculosis)
American Name: Consumption
Modern Disease Name: Tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Sanatorium rest cure, alpine air therapy, cod liver oil, bloodletting, gold salts, counter-irritant chest plasters, and therapeutic pneumothorax (collapsing the lung).
20th Century Treatments: Streptomycin (1943), Para-aminosalicylic acid (PAS), Isoniazid (1952), Rifampicin (1965), and BCG vaccination.
Current Standard Treatments: Multi-drug therapy regimen (Rifampin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol - RIPE protocol); specialized regimens for MDR-TB and XDR-TB incorporating Bedaquiline and Linezolid.
2. Phthisis
American Name: Phthisis
Modern Disease Name: Pulmonary Tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Inhalations of creosote or tar vapors, opium preparations for cough suppression, milk diets, and cauterization.
20th Century Treatments: Surgical thoracoplasty, streptomycin, isoniazid, and ethambutol.
Current Standard Treatments: Standard 6-month combination antibiotic protocol (RIPE regimen) with directly observed therapy (DOT).
3. King's Evil
American Name: King's evil
Modern Disease Name: Tuberculous Cervical Lymphadenitis (Scrofula)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: The "Royal Touch" (monarchs touching the scrofulous swellings), topical iodine tinctures, surgical excision of swollen neck glands, and poultices.
20th Century Treatments: Surgical debridement combined with early anti-tuberculous chemotherapy.
Current Standard Treatments: First-line systemic anti-TB pharmacotherapy (RIPE) for 6 months; surgical excision reserved strictly for refractory or suppurative cases.
4. Brain Fever
American Name: Brain fever
Modern Disease Name: Acute Meningitis (Neisseria meningitidis / Streptococcus pneumoniae)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Cold ice packs applied to the shaved scalp, aggressive venesection (bloodletting), leeching behind the ears, mercury subchloride (calomel), and lumbar punctures for fluid decompression.
20th Century Treatments: Flexner’s antimeningococcal serum (1907), Sulfonamides (1930s), Penicillin G (1940s), and polysaccharide vaccines.
Current Standard Treatments: High-dose intravenous third-generation cephalosporins (Ceftriaxone or Cefotaxime) plus Vancomycin, adjunctive Dexamethasone, and conjugated meningococcal vaccines (MenACWY / MenB).
5. Lung Fever / Winter Fever
American Name: Lung fever / winter fever
Modern Disease Name: Lobar Pneumonia (Streptococcus pneumoniae)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Cupping, venesection, antimony emetics, mustard plasters applied to the chest, and turpentine stupes.
20th Century Treatments: Type-specific antipneumococcal serum therapy (1910s–1930s), Sulfapyridine (M&B 693), Penicillin, and 23-valent polysaccharide vaccines (PPSV23).
Current Standard Treatments: Beta-lactam antibiotics (Amoxicillin or Ceftriaxone) combined with Macrolides (Azithromycin) or respiratory Fluoroquinolones; Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccines (PCV15/PCV20).


Chapter 2: Throat Disorders and Severe Febrile Plagues
6. Throat Distemper / Sore Throat Distemper
American Name: Throat distemper / sore throat distemper
Modern Disease Name: Diphtheria (Corynebacterium diphtheriae)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Caustic silver nitrate swabbed onto the throat membrane, tracheotomy without anesthesia, blow-pipes with powdered alum, and lime-water inhalations.
20th Century Treatments: Behring & Kitasato’s Diphtheria Antitoxin (DAT) (1890s), Diphtheria Toxoid immunization (Ramon, 1920s), and Penicillin/Erythromycin.
Current Standard Treatments: Immediate administration of Equine Diphtheria Antitoxin (DAT), IV Erythromycin or Penicillin G, strict respiratory isolation, and DTaP/Tdap vaccination protocols.
7. Malignant Sore Throat
American Name: Malignant sore throat
Modern Disease Name: Severe Diphtheria / Gangrenous Pharyngitis
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Gargles of port wine and bark (quinine), gargling with vinegar and salt, external blister packs applied to the neck.
20th Century Treatments: High-dose equine antitoxin neutralization and systemic penicillin therapy.
Current Standard Treatments: Early antitoxin administration, intensive care airway management, and targeted antibacterial therapy.
8. Bladder in Throat
American Name: Bladder in throat
Modern Disease Name: Diphtheritic Pseudomembranous Croup
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Manual scraping or mechanical removal of pseudomembranes using forceps, emetics to induce violent vomiting to clear airways.
20th Century Treatments: Intubation, diphtheria antitoxin, and parenteral penicillin.
Current Standard Treatments: Airway maintenance via endotracheal intubation, DAT neutralization, and systemic antibiotics.
9. Membranous Croup
American Name: Membranous croup
Modern Disease Name: Laryngeal Diphtheria / Severe Infectious Croup
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Steam tents, croup kettles, emetic syrup of ipecac, and throat lancing.
20th Century Treatments: O'Dwyer intubation tubes, diphtheria antitoxin, nebulized epinephrine, and corticosteroids.
Current Standard Treatments: Dexamethasone, nebulized racemic epinephrine, diphtheria antitoxin (if diphtheritic), and supportive oxygenation.
10. Ague / Cold Plague / Congestive Chills
American Name: Ague / cold plague / congestive chills
Modern Disease Name: Malaria (Plasmodium species)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Peruvian Cinchona bark powder, raw Quinine extraction, Fowler’s solution (arsenic), and draining of swamp vapors (miasma theory).
20th Century Treatments: Synthetic Chloroquine, Primaquine, Mepacrine, and vector control (DDT spraying).
Current Standard Treatments: Artemisinin-based Combination Therapies (ACTs - e.g., Artemether-Lumefantrine), IV Artesunate for severe malaria, and RTS,S/R21 malaria vaccines.


Chapter 3: Enteric, Vector-Borne, and Spirocheta Infections
11. Bilious Fever
American Name: Bilious fever
Modern Disease Name: Typhoid Fever (Salmonella Typhi) / Hepatitis
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Calomel (mercury), purgatives, cold sponges, turpentine stupes applied to the abdomen, and liquid whey diets.
20th Century Treatments: Wright’s killed typhoid vaccine (1896), Chloramphenicol (1948), and Ampicillin.
Current Standard Treatments: Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin), Ceftriaxone, or Azithromycin; Typhoid Vi conjugate vaccines; supportive rehydration.
12. Jail Fever / Camp Fever / Ship Fever
American Name: Jail fever / camp fever / ship fever
Modern Disease Name: Epidemic Typhus (Rickettsia prowazekii)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Delousing with vinegar/kerosene, ventilation, isolation, wine and camphor stimulants, and vinegar-soaked rags on the forehead.
20th Century Treatments: DDT louse powder delousing, Tetracycline (1950s), and Chloramphenicol.
Current Standard Treatments: Oral or IV Doxycycline; strict vector/louse control hygiene measures.
13. Canine Madness / Hydrophobia
American Name: Canine madness / hydrophobia
Modern Disease Name: Rabies (Lyssavirus)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Cauterization of bite wounds with boiling oil or red-hot iron, madstones applied to the wound, and restraint.
20th Century Treatments: Pasteur’s live-attenuated spinal cord vaccine (1885), Duck embryo vaccine, and Human Rabies Immune Globulin (HRIG).
Current Standard Treatments: Immediate wound washing, Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) combining Human Rabies Immune Globulin (HRIG) on Day 0 and 4 doses of modern cell-culture Rabies Vaccine.
14. French Pox / Bad Blood / Great Pox
American Name: French pox / bad blood / great pox
Modern Disease Name: Syphilis (Treponema pallidum)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Mercury inunctions (salivation therapy), mercury vapor baths, guaiacum wood infusions, and "fever therapy" (malariotherapy - Wagner-Jauregg).
20th Century Treatments: Ehrlich's Salvarsan / Arsphenamine (1910), Neosalvarsan, and Penicillin G (1943).
Current Standard Treatments: Parenteral Benzathine Penicillin G (single dose for early syphilis; weekly doses for late/latent syphilis); Doxycycline for penicillin-allergic patients.
15. Lockjaw
American Name: Lockjaw
Modern Disease Name: Tetanus (Clostridium tetani)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Cauterization of dirty wounds, laudanum (opium) for spasms, chloral hydrate, and warm baths.
20th Century Treatments: Tetanus Antitoxin (TAT) (1890), Tetanus Toxoid vaccine (1920s), curare muscle relaxants, and mechanical ventilation.
Current Standard Treatments: Human Tetanus Immune Globulin (HTIG), Tetanus Toxoid immunization (Tdap/Td), Metronidazole or Penicillin G, IV Diazepam/Magnesium sulfate for severe spasms, and surgical wound debridement.


Chapter 4: Viral Hemorrhagic Fevers, Paralysis, and Toxin Envenomation
16. Breakbone Fever
American Name: Breakbone fever
Modern Disease Name: Dengue (Orthoflavivirus)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Emetics, diaphoresis (inducing sweating), cinchona bark for pain, and bloodletting.
20th Century Treatments: Oral rehydration therapy, intravenous fluids for Dengue Shock Syndrome, and Paracetamol (avoiding aspirin).
Current Standard Treatments: Isotonic IV fluid resuscitation, blood transfusion monitoring, avoidance of NSAIDs/aspirin, and Dengue vaccines (Dengvaxia/Qdenga).
17. La Grippe / Grip
American Name: La grippe / grip
Modern Disease Name: Influenza
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Quinine pills, hot toddies (whiskey, honey, lemon), mustard plasters, Dover's powder (opium + ipecac), and bed rest.
20th Century Treatments: Inactivated Influenza Vaccines (1940s), Amantadine (1960s), and Rimantadine.
Current Standard Treatments: Neuraminidase inhibitors (Oseltamivir/Zanamivir), Cap-dependent endonuclease inhibitors (Baloxavir marboxil), annual quadrivalent influenza vaccines.
18. American Plague / Yellow Jack / Bronze John / Dock Fever
American Name: American plague / yellow jack / bronze John / dock fever
Modern Disease Name: Yellow Fever (Orthoflavivirus)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Aggressive purging with calomel and jalap, venesection, quarantine of ships, mosquito smudges, and lemonade/barley water.
20th Century Treatments: Max Theiler’s 17D live attenuated Yellow Fever Vaccine (1937) and supportive fluid therapy.
Current Standard Treatments: Supportive fluid management, treatment of coagulopathies and liver failure, and 17D single-dose vaccination.
19. Black Vomit
American Name: Black vomit
Modern Disease Name: Terminal Yellow Fever Hemorrhagic Phase
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Ice chips swallowed whole, stomach poultices, opiates, and turpentine enemata.
20th Century Treatments: Blood transfusions, vitamin K, and IV fluids.
Current Standard Treatments: Critical care unit support, fresh frozen plasma/blood transfusions, renal dialysis, and hemodialysis.
20. Chin Cough / Whooping Cough
American Name: Chin cough / whooping cough
Modern Disease Name: Pertussis (Bordetella pertussis)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Syrup of squill, belladonna extracts, change of air (mountain/sea air), and suffocating burning sulfur vapor baths.
20th Century Treatments: Whole-cell pertussis vaccines (1930s), Erythromycin, and Acellular Pertussis vaccines (DTaP).
Current Standard Treatments: Macrolide antibiotics (Azithromycin or Clarithromycin) to clear nasal shedding; supportive oxygen/suctioning; DTaP/Tdap vaccination.
21. Infantile Paralysis
American Name: Infantile paralysis
Modern Disease Name: Poliomyelitis (Polio enterovirus)
Time of Discovery / Historical Treatments: Splinting and immobilization of limbs, plaster casts, strychnine injections, and Sister Kenny's hot packs and passive range-of-motion physical therapy.
20th Century Treatments: Drinker's Iron Lung respirator (1928), Salk's Inactivated Polio Vaccine (IPV, 1955), and Sabin's Oral Polio Vaccine (OPV, 1961).
Current Standard Treatments: Preventive immunization (IPV/OPV), non-invasive positive pressure ventilation, physical therapy, and orthopedic corrective management.


Conclusion & Comprehensive Availability
This complementary manuscript establishes the parallel history of therapeutics across all 53 disease entities, aligning with the biological classifications defined in The Mycelial Transposition.
